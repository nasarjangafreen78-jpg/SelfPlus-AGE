What is SelfPlus AGE+?

AGE+ is a dietary supplement sold under the SelfPlus Supplements brand and marketed for “healthy aging support.” Its star ingredient is resveratrol, a plant polyphenol found in grapes and berries, and in much higher concentrations in the root of Japanese knotweed (Polygonum cuspidatum). Knotweed root is the raw material most supplement makers use to get resveratrol in bulk, and that is the source here.

The product comes as a 30-capsule bottle, with a suggested serving of one capsule per day. It is sold through ClickBank, which handles payment and order support, while the brand handles product questions.

Why this matters: some products print a big number on the front that refers to the raw extract, not the active compound. AGE+ spells out both on the panel: 250 mg of extract and 50% trans-resveratrol. That is straightforward labeling, and it’s what you want to see.

One more note: AGE+ is often described as a “single ingredient” formula. More precisely, it has one active ingredient (knotweed root extract) plus a handful of standard fillers and flow agents. If you have a rice or lemongrass sensitivity, read the panel before ordering.

What does resveratrol research actually show?

Resveratrol has been studied for more than two decades. The manufacturer cites a 2024 systematic review that identified 194 human clinical studies, and links to research on oxidative stress markers, glucose and insulin response, and cardiovascular and cellular health. You can read the sources yourself:

  • Research linked by the manufacturer on oxidative stress (PubMed)
  • Research linked by the manufacturer on glucose and insulin (PubMed)
  • Systematic review linked by the manufacturer on cardiovascular and cellular health (MDPI)

Here is the balanced reading. “Studied” is not the same as “proven.” Human trials differ widely in dose, duration, participant health and the form of resveratrol used, and results are mixed. Resveratrol is also absorbed and cleared quickly by the body, which is a known challenge in this field. Treat it as a supplement some people choose to include in a wellness routine, not as a treatment for any condition.

How to judge any resveratrol supplement

Whether you pick AGE+ or something else, run any resveratrol product through this checklist:

  • Form: look for trans-resveratrol, the form most discussed in research.
  • Amount of active compound: check both the extract weight and the percentage. A big extract number can hide a small amount of actual resveratrol.
  • Source: Japanese knotweed root is common and well established for extraction.
  • Other ingredients: fewer is simpler, but know what is in there if you have allergies.
  • Third-party testing: independent lab verification is a plus. AGE+ doesn’t advertise it on the pages we reviewed, so ask support if this matters to you.
  • Refund policy: a real, written guarantee lowers your risk.
  • Price per month: compare cost per 30 days of the same active dose, not bottle prices.

By these measures AGE+ does well on label clarity and refund terms, and is less competitive on price.

Safety: who should check with a doctor first

Talk with your physician or pharmacist before starting any supplement, especially if you:

  • are pregnant, planning pregnancy or nursing
  • take prescription medication, particularly blood thinners or antiplatelet drugs
  • have a medical condition, or surgery coming up
  • have a hormone-sensitive health condition
  • have allergies to ingredients on the label

Resveratrol can interact with certain medications. This list is not complete, so let your healthcare provider guide you. Don’t exceed the serving on the label, and stop use and seek advice if you notice anything unusual.
